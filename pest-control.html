Common questions

Pest questions we hear every week

Can carpenter ants harm me?

Carpenter ants do not sting and are not known to cause human disease. However, they have large mouthparts and can pinch or break the skin — and they can release formic acid, which makes the pinch hurt more.

Do carpenter ants cause damage?

Yes. Carpenter ants chew and excavate wood to make galleries for their nesting colony — in new and old timber, hollow-core doors, foam insulation, even furniture. They don't eat the wood, but an established colony left untreated can cause significant damage over time. Not sure whether it's ants or termites? A termite inspection settles it.

What are ticks?

Ticks are small arachnids that need blood meals to complete their life cycle. There are over 800 species worldwide, but two families — hard ticks and soft ticks — are known to transmit disease to humans. If you've been bitten, always check with your physician, and call us to treat the yard.

Why are crickets so loud?

That cricket chirping away in your house is probably a male — most female crickets lack the sound-making wing structures. Want the concert over? We can help.

Can spiders harm me?

Most spiders you'll find in NJ homes — yellow sac, cellar, and house spiders — can't even pierce human skin. They're a nuisance rather than a threat: no damage to your home, just unsightly webbing. Seeing more than you'd like? We can clear them out.
